Welcome to PUP
A. Mabini Campus

Started back on October 17, 1904 by the American colonial government as the Manila Business School, this campus eventually evolved into the Philippine College of Commerce (PCC). Later on, during Dr. Nemesio Prudente's reorganization of the university in 1988, the grounds were officially renamed after Apolinario Mabini. Spanning across Santa Mesa along Anonas Street, these grounds hold layers of rich academic and political history—including celebrating our Centennial Year in 2004, alongside many buildings deeply tied to student movements during the Marcos era.

This self-guided tour takes you through the key historic landmarks, the day-to-day facilities students actually hang out in, and a couple of standout alumni who've brought major pride to the university.

Historic Landmarks

Start Here
PUP Main Gate, A. Mabini Campus

The Mural & Dambana ng Kabayanihan

The brass mural relief, titled "Consolidated Growth Through Education," was inaugurated in 1974 for PCC's (now PUP's) 70th anniversary, sculpted by Eduardo Castrillo — the same artist behind the EDSA People Power Monument. Right beside it at the gate is a separate memorial called "Dambana ng Kabayanihan" (Shrine of Heroism), dedicated to the youth leaders who opposed the Marcos dictatorship, many of whom came from PUP. So the mural itself isn't named "Dambana" — that's the adjacent monument, but the two sit together at the gate and often get referenced as one gate ensemble.

Main Gate Entrance

The Pylon

The three columns of the PUP Pylon symbolize Truth, Excellence and Wisdom. Initially representing "the true, the good, and the beautiful," its symbolism evolved, and today it anchors PUP's own APO fraternity tradition, the annual Pylon Run.

East of Freedom Plaza

Apolinario Mabini Shrine

A bamboo-and-nipa house originally in Nagtahan, Manila, where Mabini first lived in 1888 and died of cholera on May 13, 1903. It was relocated in the 1930s, moved again to Malacañang Park in 1960 under architect Juan Nakpil, and finally to the PUP campus in 2010 (other sources cite 2008) to protect it from an MMDA flood-control project. It now houses the Museo ni Apolinario Mabini.

A. Mabini Campus · formerly Freedom Hall / Unyon ng Mag-aaral Bldg.

Charlie Del Rosario Bldg.

Named for Carlos "Charlie" del Rosario, a 27-year-old Political Science and History instructor at the Philippine College of Commerce and a ranking member of the Movement for a Democratic Philippines and former KM general secretary, who disappeared on March 19, 1971 and is considered the first desaparecido/victim of the Marcos regime. A 2025 document discovery confirmed he was abducted, tortured, and killed by the Presidential Security Unit

A. Mabini Campus, Oval

Gabriela Silang Bldg.

Named after the Ilocano revolutionary heroine Gabriela Silang; the building functions as a hall for student organizations and offices. It became a flashpoint in 2017 when the administration and student activists clashed over the closure of Gabriela Silang Hall, a two-story building for student activities, with student offices and "tambayans" shut and students forcibly removed by security.

Student Movements & Milestones

Voices of the Iskolar
2010

"Burning Chairs" Protest

Students walked out of classes and burned old, damaged armchairs to protest a proposed 2,000% tuition hike. Nearly a thousand students threw chairs, tables, and papers from the Main Building's upper floors to the ground before setting them on fire.

2026

PUP CCIS Walkout Against Unfair Hands-On Exams

CCIS students staged a walkout after the college abruptly changed the schedule for their major hands-on exams in the last week of May 2026 despite the original dates having been posted back in February. The changes collided with students' already-set schedules for final projects and exams in their other subjects, especially their minors. Students raised their voices and concerns but to no avail. In response, they collectively walked out, not just to protest the scheduling conflict, but to assert that student concerns can't be dismissed.

Marina Summers

Marina Guinto Alabado, a PUP BA graduate known as Marina Summers, became the first transgender woman to host the Miss Universe Philippines coronation night in 2026. She was also the first drag queen to perform at the pageant in 2024, and a finalist on RuPaul's Drag Race: UK vs the World.

Joymayanne "Joy" Barcoma, a PUP Broadcasting alumna and former Iskolar ng Bayan, won Miss Philippines Earth 2025 and placed Top 8 at Miss Earth 2025. Back in her college days she already won Miss PASUC National 2017, representing PUP.
